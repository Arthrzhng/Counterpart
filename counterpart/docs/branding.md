**Counterpart**

Branding & Go-to-Market Strategy

*Revised — May 2026*

*This document supersedes the earlier branding strategy. The earlier version led with anti-sycophancy as the primary positioning. This version leads with deep personalization ("AI that actually knows you") because the commercial evidence for personalization-led products is significantly stronger than for honesty-led products. Anti-sycophancy becomes one expression of personalization, not the headline. The goal — maximize signups for behavioral data collection — is unchanged.*

# 1. What changed and why

## 1.1 The earlier framing was too narrow

The earlier strategy positioned Counterpart as the AI that won't flatter you. That framing has three problems for a data-collection-first product:

- It is negative — defined by what we won't do — which is harder to market than positive features.

- The market evidence for honesty-led AI is weak: Inflection's Pi (closest analog) effectively collapsed; Character.ai, Replika, and ChatGPT all grew on the opposite positioning.

- It implies a niche, occasional-use advisor product rather than a daily driver. Daily drivers collect more data faster.

## 1.2 The new positioning

Counterpart is the AI assistant that actually knows you. The same things you use ChatGPT for — explanations, code help, writing, brainstorming, decisions — but personalized in a way ChatGPT structurally cannot match. The longer you use it, the more it understands how you think, learn, and decide. Including, where it helps, pushing back on your reasoning instead of agreeing with you.

This framing has four advantages:

- Positive — promises something users get, not something we refuse to do

- Functional — the value is concrete (better answers calibrated to you), not philosophical

- Daily-driver compatible — users don't need to change their behavior, the product just works better than what they currently use

- Structurally defensible — incumbents at 800M user scale cannot do deep personalization without rebuilding

## 1.3 What this means for marketing

Lead with personalization. Use anti-sycophancy as a proof point, not a headline. The hierarchy of messages is:

- Hero claim: AI that learns how you think and gets sharper the more you use it.

- Functional proof: Calibrated to your level. Adapted to your style. Knows what you're working on.

- Differentiator proof: Notices what you're missing — including where you'd usually rationalize past it.

- Mission underneath: We give you what you need, not just what you want.

The mission line shows up in About pages, founder essays, and YC pitches — not on the homepage.

# 2. Positioning

## 2.1 The category we are creating

Not "a better ChatGPT." Not "honest AI." The category is personal AI — the AI that knows you specifically, distinct from the generic AI that knows everyone equally.

Personal AI vs general AI is a real, defensible category split:

|  | **General AI (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini)** | **Personal AI (Counterpart)** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Optimizes for | broad acceptability across 800M users | depth of fit with one user |
| Voice | neutral, friendly, generic | calibrated to you specifically |
| Memory | facts about you | how you think and decide |
| Value over time | stays roughly the same | compounds — gets better as it learns you |
| Best at | any single question | your recurring questions |

## 2.2 Positioning statement

*For knowledge workers, founders, and high-achieving students who use AI every day, Counterpart is the personal AI assistant that learns how you think. Unlike general assistants that give everyone the same answer, Counterpart adapts to your level, your style, and your reasoning patterns — and the longer you use it, the more useful it becomes.*

## 2.3 Hero copy options

Each tested against the new framing. Pick one and commit; do not try to use all four.

- "The AI that actually knows you. Calibrated to your thinking, your level, your style — and gets sharper the more you use it."

- "Your AI, not everyone's. Counterpart learns how you think and adapts to it. Most assistants give the same answer to everyone — Counterpart gives you yours."

- "AI that compounds. Every conversation makes the next one better. Most assistants reset; Counterpart learns."

- "Generic AI plateaus. Personal AI compounds. Counterpart is the AI assistant that genuinely knows you — your thinking, your work, your gaps."

Recommendation: option 3 ("AI that compounds") leads with the strongest functional claim. Use the others as supporting copy on the landing page.

## 2.4 Subhead and proof points

Whichever hero you pick, support it with three concrete proof points users can immediately understand:

- Knows your level — explanations calibrated to what you already understand, not what the average user understands

- Adapts to your style — worked examples vs first principles, terse vs detailed, formal vs casual

- Notices your patterns — surfaces what you're missing, including where you tend to rationalize past it

# 3. Naming

*The naming problem from the earlier document is unchanged. "Counterpart" has a heavy commercial trademark conflict with Counterpart (Agentic Insurance) and weak SEO. Treat it as an internal codename and plan to rename before public scale.*

## 3.1 Why the name still needs to change

- Active trademark in AI-adjacent classes (Counterpart insurtech is an AI insurance product)

- "Counterpart" is descriptive of "a parallel/equivalent" — the cognitive-complement story it implies is now the wrong story (we're not inverting the user, we're learning them)

- SEO is dominated by the insurtech company

- The name evokes opposition/inversion, which is misaligned with the new "learns and adapts" positioning

## 3.2 What the name should signal under the new positioning

The earlier naming brief asked for names that evoked discernment, friction, or completion. Under the new positioning, the name should signal:

- Personal connection / belonging to you ("yours", "with you", "part of you")

- Adaptation / learning / growth

- Sharpness or precision (intelligence specific to you)

Three name buckets aligned to the new positioning:

- **Humanized first names** — Ada, Rune, Alma, Linus, Mira. Signals partner / companion. Cheap to trademark, evolves easily.

- **Invented words** — Lume, Klio, Aris, Vela, Thren. Cleanest legal slate, easy SEO, blank semantic canvas.

- **Possessive/pronoun-adjacent** — Mine, Yours, Self, Solo, Kin. Risky for trademark but signals the personal-AI category directly.

Strong recommendation: invent a word or use a humanized first name. Avoid descriptive English nouns; they have the same problem "Counterpart" has.

# 4. Audience and growth strategy

## 4.1 Why personalization positioning works for a wider audience

Anti-sycophancy positioning would have appealed mostly to AI-skeptical power users — a vocal but small slice. Personalization positioning is mass-market compatible because everyone already wants their tools to know them better. Spotify, TikTok, and Cursor have all proven the audience for "this thing actually knows me" is large and willing to switch.

## 4.2 Audience priority order

All four target groups stay, but the priority order changes under the new positioning:

| **Priority** | **Audience** | **Why this order** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | High-achieving students | Heaviest AI users by hours/day; most discerning about explanation quality; vocal on social; cheap to reach via TikTok/X |
| 2 | Founders | Already on X/HN; receptive to build-in-public; high lifetime value; can amplify the brand |
| 3 | Knowledge workers | Largest TAM but slower to adopt; reach via LinkedIn once 1+2 have generated proof |
| 4 | Decision-makers / execs | Last; reached through case studies generated by 1–3 |

The product itself doesn't change across audiences — the same personal AI, just with different example use cases on different surfaces. Students see study-help demos, founders see decision-support demos, knowledge workers see productivity demos.

## 4.3 The data-maximization strategy

Goal: maximum signups, fast, with retention strong enough that the data is useful. Five principles:

- Open access from day one. No long waitlist. Behavioral data is the moat; do not gate it.

- Generous free tier. Mirror Cursor and Granola: free is real, premium upgrade is sharp. Free users are training data.

- Onboarding that surfaces personalization fast. A 3–5 minute structured first conversation that produces a visibly specific profile, so users feel the value before they have a long history.

- Imported context. Let users connect existing notes, journals, or chat histories. Bootstrap the model from what already exists about them — turns the cold-start problem into a warm-start product.

- Compounding-value moments. After 10 messages, surface "here's what Counterpart has learned about you so far." After 50 messages, surface "here's how your conversations have improved." Make the personalization visible so users feel the lock-in.

## 4.4 Channel strategy by audience

| **Audience** | **Primary channel** | **Content that works** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Students | TikTok, X, Discord, university Reddit | Study-help demos, "watch ChatGPT vs Counterpart explain X", before/after profiles |
| Founders | X, Hacker News, IndieHackers | Build-in-public threads, decision-quality demos, technical posts on the personalization architecture |
| Knowledge workers | LinkedIn, X, AI newsletters (Lenny's, Every, Ben's Bites) | "AI that actually knows my work" case studies, productivity demos |
| Decision-makers | LinkedIn, executive newsletters, podcasts | Long-form essays on the personal-AI category, founder interviews |

## 4.5 The single most important content type

A 30–60 second screen recording showing Counterpart's response to the same question for two different users — one a beginner, one an expert; or one with project context, one without. The visible difference between the responses is the entire pitch in 30 seconds.

This is your highest-ROI content. Make 5–10 of these in the first month. They go on every social channel, the landing page, and the YC application demo.

# 5. Founder-led growth

## 5.1 Still the entire engine

The earlier conclusion holds: for a solo student founder, founder-led content is the primary growth engine for the first 6+ months. What changes under the new positioning is the content itself.

## 5.2 The new content arc

The earlier strategy framed your content as "the case against agreeable AI." Under the new positioning, the content arc is broader and more constructive:

- **Phase 1 (months 1–2): The personal AI thesis. **Why generic AI plateaus and personal AI compounds. Why this matters now. What's structurally different about it. One manifesto-length essay; ten threads building the argument.

- **Phase 2 (months 2–4): Building in public. **Weekly metrics, screenshots of the system inferring something correctly about a user, surprises in the data. Specific cases where Counterpart was visibly better than ChatGPT for a specific user.

- **Phase 3 (months 4–6): Earned authority. **Patterns you've noticed across users, what personalization does and doesn't help with, the limits of the approach. Honest essays — including what's not working — build credibility faster than wins-only marketing.

## 5.3 Content cadence

| **Cadence** | **Format** | **Channel** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1× per quarter | Long-form essay (1,500–3,000 words) | Substack/personal blog → cross-posted X, LinkedIn, HN |
| 1× per week | Build-in-public thread or demo video | X primary, LinkedIn repurpose |
| 3× per week | Short post, reply, or screenshot | X primary |
| 1× per month | Podcast appearance or guest essay | AI newsletters, founder podcasts |

Realistic time investment: 5–8 hours/week, batched into two sessions. Record one talk-aloud session per week and atomize via AI into multiple posts.

# 6. Onboarding and product-led growth

Under the new positioning, the onboarding is doing more work than the earlier strategy assumed. It is not just signup — it is the moment users feel personalization for the first time.

## 6.1 The onboarding loop

- Signup with email or Google SSO — under 30 seconds

- Three structured questions that surface explicit context: what do you do, what are you working on this week, how do you typically prefer information presented

- First conversation with the AI that visibly uses what you just told it — calibrated tone, calibrated detail, references the project you mentioned

- After 10 messages, a visible "profile preview" — "here's what Counterpart understands about you so far" — with edit and delete options

- Optional: connect an existing source (notes, prior chat history, doc) to bootstrap the model with more depth

## 6.2 The compounding-value moments

Personalization is invisible if users don't see it working. Surface the depth at three points:

- After 10 messages: profile preview

- After 50 messages: "here's how your conversations have evolved" — show the user something concrete the AI has noticed

- After 200 messages: a deeper synthesis — patterns, growth, recurring themes — as a shareable, downloadable artifact

These moments serve three purposes: they make users feel the value, they generate shareable content (the 200-message synthesis is a natural "I have to show this to someone" moment), and they retain users by raising the cost of switching to a generic AI that knows nothing about them.

## 6.3 The privacy and transparency layer

Personalization-led positioning requires user trust at a higher level than the earlier framing. Specifically:

- Profile data is viewable and editable from settings, always

- Delete account is one click and actually deletes (including Mem0 and embedding stores)

- No third-party data sharing, ever, full stop, marketed as a positive

- UK/EU users: full GDPR compliance from day one (you are UK-based, so this matters for your local market and YC's enterprise positioning)

Trust is part of the brand. Users will only let an AI know them deeply if they trust the company behind it. Make this explicit in the marketing — the privacy posture is not a footnote, it's a feature.

# 7. YC application strategy

## 7.1 What's stronger about the new pitch

The earlier framing — anti-sycophancy AI — is intellectually interesting but harder to pitch as a venture-scale opportunity. "AI that won't flatter you" sounds like a niche product.

The new framing — personal AI — is structurally larger and maps to YC's published preferences:

- It's a category claim, not a feature claim — investors fund categories

- The behavioral data flywheel is a clear, defensible moat — directly addresses the "just a wrapper" objection

- It's compatible with YC's recent emphasis on AI products that compound user value — Garry Tan's commentary on what they look for

- It has obvious scaling paths — consumer free tier → professional → enterprise (sales coaching, writing assistance, decision support)

## 7.2 The YC narrative

Apply with this story:

- ChatGPT and Claude have plateaued — they are the same product for everyone, and that is structural, not fixable at their scale.

- The next generation of AI is personal — AI that compounds in usefulness as it learns you, the way a good therapist or trusted advisor does.

- This requires a different architecture (deep user modeling, not just memory of facts) that incumbents cannot build without rebuilding.

- We've built it. Here's the demo. Here's the retention data. Here's what users say after 30 days.

## 7.3 What to have ready

- A 30-second screen recording showing two users get measurably different answers from the same prompt

- 50–500 active users with strong qualitative feedback ("I use this 3x/day instead of ChatGPT for X")

- Week-over-week retention numbers — D7 above 30%, D30 above 15% is competitive

- A clear statement of the data flywheel as a moat: every interaction trains a deeper model of how this user thinks

- The founder insight: why you, why now, why this is obvious to you and not to others

# 8. Brand identity

## 8.1 Tone

Under the earlier positioning, the recommended tone was direct, slightly contrarian, dry. Under the new positioning, the tone shifts: still direct, but warmer. We are the AI that knows you, not the AI that argues with you.

- Direct without being aggressive

- Confident, not defensive about the category

- Specific over abstract — "we noticed your last three messages assumed X" beats "we're more honest"

- Slightly literary — the audience is sophisticated; reward intelligence in copy

- Never preachy, never moralizing about competitors

## 8.2 Visual

ChatGPT and Claude have converged on "soft tech utopian" — friendly gradients, pastel rounded shapes, warm onboarding voice. They need to feel safe to a billion users. We don't. The visual language should feel:

- Closer to Linear or Stripe than to Calm or Headspace

- Dark mode native, with one strong accent color (not blue — too associated with incumbents)

- Editorial typography — Inter, Söhne, GT America, or similar disciplined sans-serif

- Minimal motion — when motion appears, it signals intentionality and precision

- Photography of humans thinking, not glowing-orb-on-blue-gradient AI clichés

## 8.3 The visual motif

Earlier brief asked for a motif of complementarity (two halves, missing piece). Under the new positioning, the motif should evoke depth, learning, and personal fit — something like:

- A growing or expanding form (representing the model deepening over time)

- Concentric layers (representing the four layers of personalization)

- A signature/fingerprint metaphor (representing personal uniqueness)

Avoid: brain icons, lightbulbs, sparkles, robot avatars. The category is human-augmented thinking, not robotic intelligence.

# 9. Risks specific to the new positioning

## 9.1 **"**Personalized**"** is a tired word

Every product claims personalization. Users have learned to discount the word.

Mitigation: never use "personalized" as the primary claim without a specific, demonstrable proof. Always pair the claim with the proof in the same sentence: "AI that knows your level — explanations match what you understand, not what the average user understands."

## 9.2 Cold-start kills the pitch

If new users open the product and it feels generic for the first 5 messages, they leave before personalization kicks in.

Mitigation: the structured onboarding (§6.1) does most of the work here. Even basic explicit context — what they're working on, what they prefer — is enough to make message 1 feel meaningfully different from ChatGPT. The architectural cold-start solutions (cluster archetypes from the classifier plan) compound this from message 5 onward.

## 9.3 Privacy fear cancels the deeper-personalization upside

If users feel watched or modeled in ways they didn't consent to, they will leave faster than for any other reason.

Mitigation: profile transparency from day one (visible, editable, deletable). Make privacy a brand feature. Pre-empt the "creepy" reaction by showing users exactly what the system knows and giving them complete control.

## 9.4 The **"**just a ChatGPT wrapper**"** critique

Personalization-led positioning still attracts this objection. The defense is the data flywheel: every user interaction makes our model deeper, in a way the underlying frontier model isn't.

Mitigation: in technical content (HN, founder essays, YC pitch), make the architecture visible — the classifier system, the user modeling, the steering layer. Not as marketing copy but as evidence that there's real engineering behind the personalization claim.

## 9.5 Engagement-vs-needs tension still exists

Optimizing for retention metrics will pull the system toward more agreeable, validating responses — which is exactly what your underlying philosophy says is bad for users. Personalization framing softens this tension but doesn't eliminate it.

Mitigation: track quality metrics alongside engagement metrics from day one. Decision quality, user-reported usefulness, growth-over-time signals. If complement-arm users churn faster but report higher quality, that's a finding to honor, not paper over.

- Commit to personal AI positioning, not anti-sycophancy. The mission underneath stays — give users what they need, not what they want — but it lives in About pages and founder essays, not the homepage.

- Rename before public launch. "Counterpart" is a working name only. Budget £300–£700 on legal clearance for the new name. Do not skip this.
