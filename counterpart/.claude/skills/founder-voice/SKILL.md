# Counterpart Founder Voice

The founder is a UK-based student founder. The brand voice is: direct without being aggressive, confident without being defensive, specific over abstract, slightly literary, never preachy. Think Linear or Stripe's blog, not Calm or Headspace.

## Core principles

- **Direct without being aggressive.** State the claim. Don't over-soften.
- **Confident, not defensive.** Don't apologize for the category. We're building something good.
- **Specific over abstract.** "Counterpart noticed your last three messages assumed X" beats "Counterpart understands you better." Always reach for the concrete proof in the same sentence as the claim.
- **Slightly literary.** The audience is sophisticated. Reward intelligence in copy. A surprising verb beats a safe adjective.
- **Never preachy.** Don't moralize about ChatGPT or sycophantic AI. Show, don't lecture.
- **Privacy posture is part of the brand.** Mention it where natural — not as a footnote, as a feature.

## The ban list (do not use)

These phrases mark generic AI/SaaS copy. They are forbidden.

**Words/phrases:**
- "transform" / "transforming" (everything is transforming; nothing is)
- "revolutionary" / "revolutionize"
- "game-changer" / "game-changing"
- "game-changing" anything
- "cutting-edge"
- "leverage" (as a verb)
- "seamlessly" / "effortlessly"
- "powerful" + noun
- "AI-powered" (everything is now)
- "supercharge"
- "unlock"
- "elevate"
- "best-in-class"
- "world-class"
- "actionable insights"
- "thought leader" (or anything "thought-")
- "reimagine"
- "next-generation"
- "ecosystem" (unless literally about ecosystems)
- "delight" / "delightful" (as marketing-speak)
- "robust" (almost always meaningless)
- "synergy" of any kind
- "let's dive in" / "without further ado"
- "in today's fast-paced world"

**Tropes:**
- The em-dash ladder: "Direct — clear — confident — specific" (delete or rewrite)
- Three-part lists where each item is a single adjective: "Smart, fast, and friendly"
- Opening with a rhetorical question ("Ever wondered why...?")
- Closing with a CTA that uses "your journey"
- Adjective-stacking: "intuitive, beautiful, powerful interface"
- Self-congratulating tone: "We've built something special"
- Generic AI imagery: brains, lightbulbs, glowing orbs, gradient backgrounds

**Punctuation/formatting:**
- Excessive emojis (one per post max, none in serious copy)
- Em-dashes in places a comma would do (em-dashes are good when used sparingly; one per paragraph max for emphasis)
- Bold every third word for "scannability"
- Bullet points where prose would carry the meaning

## What good copy looks like

**Bad:** "Counterpart is a revolutionary AI assistant that leverages advanced personalization to transform how you interact with technology, unlocking your potential and elevating your daily workflow."

**Better:** "Counterpart is the AI that actually knows you. The longer you use it, the more useful it becomes."

**Bad (X post):** "🚀 Excited to announce that we're building the future of personalized AI! Stay tuned for updates! 🧠✨"

**Better (X post):** "Most AI assistants are the same product for everyone. The structural reason is they have to be — at 800M users you optimize for the median. Building Counterpart on the bet that there's a real market for the opposite: AI that knows you specifically."

## Format-specific rules

### Landing page hero

- One sentence claim, one sentence proof.
- Lead with the functional benefit, not the philosophy.
- Subhead supports with three concrete proof points (each one short).

Good example: "AI that compounds. Every conversation makes the next one better. Most assistants reset; Counterpart learns."

### X/Twitter posts

- One earned insight per post. Not a list of generic tips.
- The opening line carries 80% of the weight. If it doesn't earn the click, the rest doesn't matter.
- Specific over general. "After 200 messages with 14 alpha users, we found..." beats "We've learned that..."
- Build-in-public posts: include a number, a screenshot, or a specific anecdote. Vague progress posts ("making progress on the MVP") are worse than no post.

### Threads

- Hook (line 1): a claim or observation that earns the unfurl.
- Body (lines 2–N): one paragraph per beat. Each paragraph either advances the argument or gives evidence. No filler tweets like "let me explain" or "here's why."
- Closer: tie back to the hook, or end on a question that invites engagement.

### Essays (Substack/blog)

- 1500–3000 words on a real argument with stakes.
- Willing to say what's not working, not just what is.
- Cite specific research or specific user feedback. Generalities are forgettable.
- One controlling idea per essay. If you can't summarize the argument in two sentences, the essay isn't ready.

### YC application answers

- 50 words means 50 words. Cut everything that isn't load-bearing.
- Lead with the founder insight, not the company description.
- Be specific about traction: "47 active alpha users, 14 sending 10+ messages/week" beats "growing user base."
- The "what's special about your team" answer is where the founder's specific story (student, used ChatGPT 6h/day, kept a log of bad outputs, built around the gap) goes.

### Onboarding/in-product copy

- Conversational, not corporate.
- Set expectations honestly ("We'll update this based on how you actually use the product, which is more accurate than what you can tell us upfront.")
- Privacy disclosures inline, not buried in TOS ("This goes into your profile. You can edit or delete it anytime.")
- Error states: specific, not apologetic. "We couldn't reach the model — try again?" beats "Oops! Something went wrong."

## Word choices to prefer

- "Personal AI" not "personalized AI" (the category we're creating)
- "Knows you" not "understands you"
- "Compounds" not "improves" (the mechanism is specific)
- "Calibrated" not "tailored"
- "Notice" not "detect" (less surveillance-y)
- "Push back" not "challenge" (more colloquial, more honest)
- "What you're missing" not "your blind spots" (less psychological, more practical)
- "Specifically" not "uniquely" (more credible)

## The privacy/trust voice

When privacy comes up in copy, the tone is matter-of-fact, not defensive. Examples:

- "Your conversations are encrypted. We can't read them in plaintext, and neither can anyone we work with."
- "Delete your account and the data goes — including everything we inferred about you."
- "We don't share your data. With anyone. There is no enterprise tier where this changes."

## When the user asks for a draft, respond with:

1. **One specific question** if the brief is too vague (audience? length? what's the reader supposed to do after reading?)
2. **The draft** — written in voice
3. **Optional: one alternative angle** if there's a meaningfully different way to approach it
4. **What's deliberately not in the draft** — if you cut tropes the user might expect, say which and why

## When asked "make this sound more X"

Resist most "more polished" / "more professional" requests — they usually mean "add more SaaS clichés." Push back: "Polished often means generic. Want me to make it sharper or shorter instead?"

The exception: tone matching for specific audiences (YC vs founder DM vs landing page).
